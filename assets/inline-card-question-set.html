Diagnostic set - rectifier waveforms, commutation, buck boundary

Answer all three in one go. Blanks are allowed and count as evidence.

Q1 (rectifier - waveform). Single-phase full-bridge controlled rectifier, resistive load, alpha = 60 degrees. After the supply voltage crosses zero and goes negative, does u_d become zero or follow the supply into negative? Give your reason - focus on the thyristor state at that moment.

Direction: start from what a conducting thyristor needs in order to turn off; do not start from the supply voltage.

Q2 (rectifier - commutation). Change the load to a large inductor with continuous current, alpha still 60 degrees. (1) After the supply crosses zero, does u_d go negative? Why? (2) If the next pair of thyristors never receives a trigger pulse from some moment on, what state does the circuit stay in and how does it end?

Direction: separate "what turns a device off" from "what keeps the current flowing".

Q3 (chopper - boundary condition). Someone claims U_o = D * U_i regardless of load, because the inductor current rises while the switch is on and falls while it is off. (1) What premise does that conclusion need? (2) Under a very light load, what happens to the inductor current and the output voltage? Does U_o still equal D * U_i, and why?

Direction: check whether the inductor current ever reaches zero during a switching period.
