Diagnostic set - float comparison, exact cases, and tolerance choice

Answer all three in one go. Blanks are allowed and count as evidence.

Q1 (representation). 0.1 + 0.2 == 0.3 is false in most languages. (1) What is 0.1 actually stored as? (2) Why does the sum miss 0.3 by a nonzero amount?

Direction: start from how a decimal fraction is written in binary, not from "computers are imprecise".

Q2 (cases). Give one case where == on floats is reliable, and one where it silently breaks. (1) The reliable case and why it is exact. (2) The case that breaks and what the failure looks like.

Direction: check whether both values are exactly representable - small integers and powers of two are.

Q3 (boundary). A teammate replaces every a == b with abs(a - b) < 1e-9. (1) What goes wrong when both values are around 1e9? (2) What goes wrong around 1e-12? What should the tolerance depend on?

Direction: ask what the tolerance should be relative to; one fixed absolute epsilon cannot be right at both ends.
